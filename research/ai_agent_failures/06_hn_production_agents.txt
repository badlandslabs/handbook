 The problem isn&#x27;t that LLMs can&#x27;t be critical, it&#x27;s that LLMs don&#x27;t have taste. It&#x27;s easy to get an LLM to give praise, and it&#x27;s easy to get an LLM to give criticism, but getting an LLM to praise good things and criticize bad things is currently impossible for non-trival inputs. That&#x27;s not say that prompting your LLM to generate criticism is useless, it&#x27;s just that any LLM prompted to generate criticism is going to criticize things are that actually fine, just like how an LLM prompted to generate praise (which is effectively the default behavior) is going to praise things that are deeply not fine. bubblyworld 12 months ago | root | parent | next [–]

Your skepticism of "llm-as-a-judge" setups is spot on. If your LLM can make mistakes&#x2F;hallucinate, then of course, your judge llm can too. In practice, you need to validate your judges and possibly adapt to your task based on sample annotated data. You might adapt them by trial and error, or prompt optimization, e.g., using DSPy [1], or learning a small correction model on top of their outputs, e.g., LLM-Rubric [2] or Prediction Powered Inference [3].
